What Thanksgiving is

The holiday, in plain terms

Thanksgiving is a national holiday in the United States, held on the fourth Thursday of November — November 26 in 2026. It is not a religious feast day and it belongs to no single community; it is a civic day off, built around one idea: stop, eat with people you care about, and say what you are grateful for.

The popular story is a three-day harvest meal in 1621 shared by Wampanoag people and English colonists who had survived their first year. Historians agree a gathering happened, and that the version most of us were taught leaves a great deal out: the Wampanoag were not guests at someone else’s table, and the years that followed were violent and dispossessing. Many Native American communities observe the day as a National Day of Mourning. Both things are part of the holiday, and it is more honest to hold them together than to pretend only one is true.

Abraham Lincoln made it a national occasion in 1863, in the middle of the Civil War, as a call to count what survived. Congress wrote the fourth-Thursday rule into law in 1941. The modern traditions are familiar: a turkey, a table that is too small, parades, football, volunteering, and the round of “what are you thankful for?” before anyone is allowed to eat.

Why Nexus celebrates it

Our reasons, not the calendar’s

Nexus is a small team and a young product. Nothing obliges us to mark an American holiday — we do it because the things the day is about are the things we are actually trying to build.

★ Gratitude is the whole point

Software is a strange gift: it only works when someone else shows up to use it. The people who open Nexus on an ordinary Tuesday are why any of this exists, and one day a year we say so out loud instead of in a changelog.

🌱 A harvest is a shipping metaphor

Our seasonal build is called Harvest Edition v6.0 for a reason. A harvest is what you get after a long season of unglamorous work — planting, waiting, fixing what broke. Shipping feels exactly like that.

💬 We are literally building a table

Chat, files, and AI in one place is our version of a table: somewhere people gather, pass things around, and stay a while. Thanksgiving is the original version of that idea, and it is a good thing to aim at.

⏰ Rest is part of the work

Small teams burn out quietly. A holiday that insists on a slow week — no launch, no scramble — is a feature, not a break from the roadmap. We come back with better judgement than we left with.

To be clear about what this is: Nexus does not sell Thanksgiving, and celebrating a holiday is not a marketing position. It is a cultural day our team marks, with the same honesty we would expect from anyone explaining a tradition they did not invent.

How the season shows up in Nexus

Small touches, no tricks

  • The harvest boot. The workspace opens with a short autumn boot sequence and a light layer of falling leaves. It is decoration, and it stays out of the way of the app underneath.
  • The Updates page. Release notes carry a “Thanksgiving 2026 — A harvest of updates” banner, with a Send thanks button if you want to tell us something good.
  • The palette. The season shifts the accent colours toward pumpkin and amber. Everything remains readable, and anyone who prefers the plain dark theme keeps it.
  • The table. The team announcement video and the seasonal release notes live on the Updates page, so you can see who is behind the product and what changed.
